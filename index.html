About

2008 – 2011

Taking it Apart

Joseph Dennis's path into programming started years before it became a career. He first learned computers from the inside out, taking machines apart to understand how they worked, and taught himself HTML and CSS in 2008. Later, it was a beginner's programming book that introduced him to how computers work from machine code up, and for the first time gave him a clear sense of direction.

The First Real Build

That direction became practical in 2011. While working at the Canada Revenue Agency he taught himself to build a fully custom reservation system with a built-in perpetual calendar in Excel using formulas & VBA; his first real project, and the start of a career spent building tools shaped around problems off-the-shelf software solved.

2015 – present

Building for Businesses

In 2015, Joseph founded Venture31, his freelance practice, where he designs and builds client websites, deploys and manages server infrastructure, and creates custom business tools. Projects include a point-of-sale system for a sports and arts studio, and a shipping cost calculator for a licensed DHL outlet that replaced a thick-binder (that house all the shipping rates) with a searchable database needing just three inputs. Across Venture31, full-time roles, and contract work, his projects span government, fitness, shipping and logistics, oil and gas, and food. What connects this work isn't the industry or the size of the job, it's the process: understanding how a team actually works, then building what fits, whether that's a single tool or an entire system.
